Rain hammered against the kitchen window as Daniel pushed the back door open with his shoulder. He was soaked to the bone, cradling a brown cardboard box under his coat as though it were made of glass. Maya did not look up from the sink; she simply turned off the tap and let the silence stretch until he spoke.

"Don't start," Daniel muttered, setting the box gently beside the toaster. "Look, I caught the last train and walked twenty minutes in this downpour—cut me some slack, will you?"

Maya wiped her hands slowly on a dish towel and turned around. "I wasn't going to say a word about the train, Dan. I'm looking at the envelope sticking out of your pocket."

"What envelope?"

"Oh, please, don't play dumb with me. You swore on Tuesday you were done cleaning up his messes, and yet here you are at midnight."
